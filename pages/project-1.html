Dhanushkodi - the lost land

A multisensory VR experience that reconstructs Dhanushkodi through memory, place and the aftermath of the 1964 cyclone.

Medium VR / 3D / Gesture Based Interactions
Role Concept / Environment / 3D Assets
Tools Unity / Blender / Arduino
Year 2025

01 / Context

Context

Dhanushkodi is one of the southernmost towns in India, which was once a thriving marketplace but was destroyed by a cyclone that hit in 1964 and was later declared a ghost town — making room for a lot of stories, religious importance and myths about the place.

The role of the player was that of an archaeologist from the Government Survey of India.

05 / Why make it multisensory?

Why make it multisensory?

  • A natural and human way of interaction.
  • Makes users feel connected to Dhanushkodi's lost memories.
  • Adds tactile / sensory feedback beyond VR hand tracking.
  • Bridges the gap between physical and virtual worlds.

06 / Making it multisensory — narrative

Making it multisensory — narrative

The main character, Arun, is a 41-year-old archaeologist from the Government Survey of India who revisits his hometown, Dhanushkodi, 36 years after the tragic event took place.

He is assigned to document the place after so many years, and in doing so also revives and witnesses some memories of his own with his father, who died during the cyclone.

His visits to specific places trigger memories and prompt the user to think about how the protagonist's life might have looked before all these years.

07 / Senses we focused on

Senses we focused on

Sight

Visuals of ruined Dhanushkodi and recreated scenes of the town before the cyclone help users see the contrast between past and present.

Touch

Gesture-based controls let users use hand movements to explore the space and reveal hidden memories, making the experience more engaging.

Hear

Ambient sounds like sea waves, wind, footsteps, conversations and storm effects create an emotional and realistic atmosphere.

Smell

Sensory photographs — of sea breeze, wet sand and the coastal environment — enhance the setting and make users feel more present in the scene.

08 / Gesture based interactions

Why chosen interactions?

To enable this interaction, we developed a gesture recognition glove using Arduino. When activated, the glove triggers a transition allowing users to witness the town as it existed before the devastating 1964 Rameshwaram cyclone. The moment the user loses contact, the immersive VR environment shifts back to the ruins.

This deliberate shift between past and present creates a powerful contrast, encouraging users to reflect on the theme of loss. The interaction is not just visual but emotional — making the user an active participant rather than a passive observer.

Touch triggers memory far more deeply than just looking at something. We wanted to make the user an active participant in the experience rather than someone who simply witnesses the visuals.

To bring a ghost town to life, we needed to physically reach into its past — so we tied that interaction to the character's own memory.

A glove makes it feel like your own hands are helping you connect with the past. It's also a more feasible and convenient option for building an immersive experience.

The glove isn't a tech choice — it's a storytelling choice to build an emotional connection.

09 / Worldbuilding

Video based storytelling

We built four main locations into our VR environment — the Church, the Stone bridge, the Boats and the Market. Understanding the importance of these places, we wove a storyline around each location to give the player a better insight into the protagonist's life.

The Church

Young Arun used to go to the church with his dad — the stereotypical Tamil dad, strict in principle yet simple and innocent at heart. Arun would ask him silly, curious questions, and his father always answered every one.

Market

Arun used to whine for things he wanted, which his mother denied — but his father would secretly buy them for him anyway, becoming his favourite friend.

Stone bridge

Here, Arun learned the seashell game and heard about the philosophies running through his family since the time of his ancestors.

Boats

Arun used to hide among the boats, and his father would come find him there — teaching him small principles of life that he never forgot, even after he grew up.

10 / Character design

Character design

The art style and colour scheme of the animation is inspired by the cartoon Malgudi Days, chosen to keep the roots and theme of the project alive through the video sequences — making the story more interesting and easier to understand from the user's perspective.
