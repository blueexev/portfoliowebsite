01 / Project brief

Project brief

What is autism?

Autism, or Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), is a condition that affects how a person thinks, learns, communicates and interacts with others. People with autism may find it hard to talk or play with others, like to do the same things in the same way, be very focused on certain interests, or get upset by loud sounds or changes in routine.

Autism is not an illness — it's a different way of experiencing the world. Every autistic person is unique, and some may need more support than others.

Young children, especially those aged 5–8 and including neurodiverse learners such as autistic children, often struggle to recognise, interpret and express complex human emotions. Traditional learning methods may not effectively engage their attention or support the development of emotional literacy in a playful, inclusive way. Additionally, most educational games tend to oversimplify emotions or lack culturally rooted, expressive frameworks.

02 / Available solutions

Available solutions

  • Emotion flashcards & charts — visual aids to teach facial expressions.
  • Therapeutic games & apps — digital or physical tools for emotion recognition.
  • Role-play & puppets — acting out emotions through play.
  • SEL programs — school-based emotional learning curricula.
  • Storybooks — children's books focused on feelings and empathy.
  • Therapy tools — used in speech or ABA therapy for emotion learning.

03 / Our solution

Our solution

Board game for autistic children

Taru is a playful learning tool designed to help autistic children understand, express and connect with emotions through the lens of the Navarasa.

Using colour-coded zones, expressive storytelling and facial-expression practice through an app, Taru supports emotional development in a gentle, engaging way.

It encourages self-awareness, builds social-emotional skills, and gives caregivers insight into a child's comfort with different emotions.

The tagline "growing with every emotion" means that Taru doesn't just teach emotions — it helps children experience, understand and grow through them.

04 / Logo and game name

Logo and game name

TARU means "tree" in Sanskrit — symbolising growth, development and strength. Just like a tree, the game supports emotional growth in children, especially autistic learners, step by step. It's deeply connected to Indian culture, aligning with the Navarasa — the nine emotions used in the game.

The board design reflects this idea: a central flower/tree motif, with each petal representing an emotion. TARU stands for a safe, rooted and nurturing space, ideal for neurodiverse emotional learning — more than a name, it represents the journey of blossoming emotionally through play.

Character design

Each Navarasa was given its own character — Adbhuta, Shanta, Veera, Hasya, Karuna, Bibhitsa, Raudra, Bhayankar and Shringar — designed to make the nine emotions easy for children to recognise and relate to.

07 / Stories and scenarios

Stories and scenarios

Each Rasa zone is paired with a small, everyday scenario that helps children connect the emotion to something familiar.

  • Listening to bedtime stories in a soft voice — Shanta
  • Blowing bubbles and seeing them float in the sunlight — Adbhuta
  • You smile and say thank you to the security guard of the building — Shringaar
  • Someone shouts at you without any reason — Raudra
  • Screaming when you see a lizard or cockroach — Bhayanaka
  • Seeing a military person for the first time in a parade — Veera

08 / Gameplay

Gameplay

Objective

Travel through all 9 Navarasa zones, explore each expression, and collect tokens by guessing emotions correctly. The player who finishes all 9 zones first and has the most tokens wins!

Setup

  • Each player gets 1 token piece and 10 tokens.
  • Place all pieces at the start.
  • The dice contains numbers 1, 2 and 3 (repeated).

Winning the game

The game ends when a player completes all 9 emotion zones. The first player to finish all zones with the most tokens is the winner.

09 / Flow of the game

Flow of the game

  1. Introduce yourselves — each player says their name or a fun fact.
  2. Roll the dice. Only if you roll a 1 can you enter the first zone (Rasa 1).
  3. Move your piece forward based on your dice number.
  4. When you enter a zone, notice the facial expression shown on the board for that Rasa (e.g. Joy, Fear, Anger).
  5. The Helper plays a short story or scenario — it matches the emotion of the zone.
  6. The player listens to the story and chooses a matching card that shows the expression or emotion.
  7. The player then reads or hears the scenario again and guesses which emotion it represents.
  8. The Helper asks: "What emotion is that?"
  9. If the player guesses correctly, they get 2 tokens.
  10. If the player guesses wrong, they give 1 token to each other player.

10 / App interface

App interface

Alongside the physical board game, Taru extends into a companion app.

App features

  • Digital version of the board game.
  • Animated stories with voice narration.
  • Touch-based emotion selection.
  • Progress tracking for emotion-recognition improvement.
  • Music therapy for calming sensations.

11 / Learnings of the board game

Learnings of the board game

Recognising expressions

Helps children identify facial expressions, tones and situational cues through storytelling and illustrations.

A non-judgmental environment

Provides a non-judgmental environment to express and guess emotions through structured play.

Non-overwhelming interactions

Controlled dice numbers, visuals and storytelling ensure non-overwhelming interactions.

12 / Conclusion

Conclusion

By completing the game, the player gains a better understanding of different emotions, learns how to express them, and builds confidence in recognising feelings in themselves and others.

  • They gain confidence in social interactions by practising expressions in a safe, playful environment.
  • The game helps improve self-regulation and emotional control, as players learn to recognise emotions before reacting.
  • It encourages empathy, making it easier to understand what others may be feeling based on expressions.
