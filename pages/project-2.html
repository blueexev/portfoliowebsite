01 / About the market

A market built around everyday exchange.

The Bopal vegetable market is a busy local ecosystem where vendors, suppliers and customers interact throughout the day. The market works through a constant exchange of products, money, information and trust.

02 / Research question

Research Question

How does the spatial layout and infrastructure of vegetable markets impact vendor operations, customer flow, user efficiency and the vendor and user experience?

03 / Methodology

Observing, asking and mapping.

The study combined observation, informal conversations and stakeholder interviews to understand how people move, communicate and make decisions within the market. We interviewed key market stakeholders, consumers, vendors and the plot owner to understand their lifestyles and current market conditions.

The vendor highlighted challenges like congestion, heat and high rent, while the consumer valued fresh produce but noted disorganization and lack of shelter. Both emphasized peak crowd times and the need for better infrastructure and comfort.

05 / Stakeholder mapping

One market, many connected roles.

Mapping the stakeholders showed how vendors, suppliers and customers depend on each other. A decision made by one stakeholder can directly affect another.

We mapped key stakeholders consumers, various vendors, the plot owner and external shopkeepers to understand their roles, behaviors, and challenges. Consumers highlighted price sensitivity, vendors shared operational struggles, the owner discussed maintenance and rent, while external shops emphasized visibility.

This helped us uncover market dynamics and interactions influencing daily operations.

06 / Insights inferred

Insight 1

Many customers prioritize price over quality, buying from multiple vendors at night when prices drop. Rising costs make them more price-sensitive, leading to vendor shifts and a preference for seasonal vegetables.


Insight 2

Stalls near entrances and parking get more sales due to better visibility and easy access, as customers prefer not to enter the market.


Insight 3

Vendors avoid government markets to maintain their freedom, avoid strict regulations and lengthy paperwork and run their businesses more easily and flexibly.

07 / A creative output

Mandi. the card game

The research was translated into a strategy-based card game where players experience the different pressures faced by people within the market.

Step into the chaotic, colorful world of the local vegetable bazaar, where every vendor, buyer, and transporter plays a vital role. Mandi. is a strategy-based card game where players become market explorers, uncovering hidden connections between stakeholders and the information they hold.

Gameplay and user testing

Gameplay and its mechanics.

Players:

3–6

Setup:

Each player starts with 3 Insight Cards and 2 Stakeholder Cards.

Goal:

Be the first to complete all your Insight objectives through matches and trades.

Gameplay:

On your turn, draw 1 Character Card from the stack. If it matches an Insight, announce the match and place it down. If not, you may offer a trade: give an Insight Card to another player in exchange for one of their Insight cards (if they're willing). Continue taking turns in this way.

Win Condition:

First player to complete all Insight matches they have (either by drawing or trading) wins the game.

09 / Zine

Documenting the market story.

After consolidating all the information gathered throughout the project, I created a zine as a creative output that highlights the most important insights. The zine focuses on concisely presenting key aspects of my market research, translating complex findings into a visually engaging and easy-to-understand format. Through this format, I aimed to combine research with storytelling, making the information more accessible, interactive and impactful.
