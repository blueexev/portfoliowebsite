Mandi — the market story

A field study of the Bopal vegetable market in Ahmedabad, translated into stakeholder research, a strategy card game and a zine.

Medium UX Research / Card Game / Zine
Role Research / Concept / Game Design
Tools Figma / Illustrator
Year 2024

01 / About the market

About the market

The Bopal Vegetable Market, located near Bhavya Park in Bopal, Ahmedabad, is a private marketplace owned by a family of ten brothers, offering a wide variety of fresh produce. With over 100 vendors, shoppers can find an extensive selection of vegetables, fruits and snacks at competitive prices. The market also provides parking facilities behind the market area.

02 / Research question

Research question

How does the spatial layout and infrastructure of vegetable markets impact vendor operations, customer flow, user efficiency and the vendor and user experience?

03 / Methodology

Methodology

We interviewed key market stakeholders — consumers, vendors and the plot owner — to understand their lifestyles and current market conditions. Vendors highlighted challenges like congestion, heat and high rent, while consumers valued fresh produce but noted disorganisation and a lack of shelter. Both emphasised peak crowd times and the need for better infrastructure and comfort.

04 / Stakeholder interviews

Manoj Bhai (vendor)

  • Age: Approx. 45
  • Role: Fruit & vegetable vendor
  • Experience: 15+ years
  • Location: Stall within a private local market
  • Working hours: 8:00 AM – 10:00 PM
  • Family involvement: Family members assist daily
  • Supply source: Fresh produce from Jamalpur Market, sourced each morning

Challenges

  • Congestion — cramped stalls and heavy footfall make movement tough during peak hours.
  • Seasonal heat — summer temperatures affect comfort and spoil produce faster.
  • Costs — pays monthly rent; amenities are maintained, but not always efficiently.

Vaishaliben (consumer)

  • Age: Mid-50s
  • Occupation: Homemaker
  • Market connection: Loyal visitor for over 15 years
  • Location: Lives nearby, walks or drives to the market
  • Shopping frequency: A few times a week
  • Preferred time: Afternoon, to avoid peak crowds and find easier parking

Challenges

  • Congestion — vendors sitting in pathways cause bottlenecks.
  • Poor infrastructure — no proper sheds; heat and rain make shopping uncomfortable.
  • Navigation issues — lack of clear structure makes movement confusing and tiring.

06 / Insights inferred

Insights inferred

  • Many customers prioritise price over quality, buying from multiple vendors at night when prices drop. Rising costs make them more price-sensitive, leading to vendor shifts and a preference for seasonal vegetables.
  • Stalls near entrances and parking get more sales due to better visibility and easy access, as customers prefer not to venture deeper into the market.
  • Vendors avoid the government market to maintain their freedom, avoid strict regulations and lengthy paperwork, and run their businesses more easily and flexibly.

07 / A creative output

Mandi. the card game

Step into the chaotic, colourful world of the local vegetable bazaar, where every vendor, buyer and transporter plays a vital role. Mandi. is a strategy-based card game where players become market explorers, uncovering hidden connections between stakeholders and the information they hold.

Facilitator

Those who enable access or support the process without directly selling.

Supplier

Those who offer goods or services for sale.

Customer

Those who purchase or request services or products.

If a player draws one of these cards, the person holding a matching card must say it out loud — and the player with the match is bound to give it up.

Gameplay and user testing

  • Players: 3–6
  • Setup: Each player starts with 3 Insight Cards and 2 Stakeholder Cards.
  • Goal: Be the first to complete all your Insight objectives through matches and trades.

Gameplay

  • On your turn, draw 1 Character Card from the stack.
  • If it matches an Insight, announce the match and place it down.
  • If not, you may offer a trade — give an Insight Card to another player in exchange for one of their Insight Cards, if they're willing.
  • Continue taking turns in this way.

Win condition

The first player to complete all their Insight matches — either by drawing or trading — wins the game.

08 / The market zine

The market zine

After consolidating all the information gathered throughout the project, I created a zine as a creative output that highlights the most important insights. The zine focuses on concisely presenting key aspects of the market research, translating complex findings into a visually engaging and easy-to-understand format.

Through this format, I aimed to combine research with storytelling — making the information more accessible, interactive and impactful.
